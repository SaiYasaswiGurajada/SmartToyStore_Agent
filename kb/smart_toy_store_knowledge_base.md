## 1. Connectivity & Technical

### 1.1 Frequency Band
Our smart toys connect over **2.4GHz Wi-Fi** (not 5GHz). If your home router broadcasts a combined SSID for both bands, you may need to temporarily split the 2.4GHz network or manually select it in your router settings during pairing, since 5GHz-only connections are not supported.

### 1.2 Connection Type (Bluetooth vs Wi-Fi)
Most of our toys use **Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)** for phone-to-toy pairing and app control, with select models (e.g., toys with live-streaming or cloud-based voice features) additionally requiring **Wi-Fi (2.4GHz)** for internet-dependent features. Basic play functions typically work over Bluetooth alone.

### 1.3 Range
Bluetooth-connected toys have an effective range of approximately **10 meters (30 feet)** in open indoor spaces, which may be reduced by walls or interference from other electronics. Wi-Fi-connected features work anywhere within your home network's coverage.

### 1.4 Multiple Toys / Simultaneous Connections
Yes, multiple toys can connect to the same account and app simultaneously. Each toy appears as a separate device in the app's device list, and can be controlled independently or as part of a group activity (for toys that support multiplayer modes).

### 1.5 Offline Functionality
Toys with core play features (sound, light, basic movement) generally work **without internet**, once the initial app pairing is complete. Features like voice assistant integration, cloud story libraries, or remote parental monitoring require an active internet connection.

### 1.6 Supported Phone OS Versions
The companion app supports **iOS 13 and above** and **Android 8.0 and above**. We recommend keeping your phone's OS and the app itself updated to the latest version for full compatibility and security patches.

## 2. Installation & Setup

### 2.1 First-Time Pairing
1. Download the companion app from the App Store or Google Play.
2. Turn on the toy and put it into pairing mode (usually a long-press on the power button until the LED blinks).
3. Open the app, select "Add New Device," and choose your toy model from the list.
4. Follow the in-app prompts to complete Bluetooth pairing and, if applicable, connect to your 2.4GHz Wi-Fi network.

### 2.2 Pairing Fails / Toy Won't Connect
If pairing fails, try the following in order:
- Confirm the toy is charged and powered on, with the LED in pairing mode.
- Confirm your phone's Bluetooth is on and not already connected to another device using the same toy.
- Move the toy closer to your phone (within 1–2 meters) during initial setup.
- Restart both the toy and the app.
- If using Wi-Fi, confirm you are connecting to a 2.4GHz network, not 5GHz.
- As a last step, factory reset the toy (see 2.4) and repeat pairing.

### 2.3 App Requirement
Yes, most features require the companion app for initial setup, even if the toy can be used standalone afterward for basic functions. Some entry-level toys do not require an app at all — check your specific product's box or manual.

### 2.4 Factory Reset
To factory reset, hold the power and pairing buttons together for **10 seconds** until the LED flashes three times, or use the "Remove Device" + "Factory Reset" option within the app's device settings menu. This will erase saved Wi-Fi credentials and app preferences.

### 2.5 What's in the Box
Standard contents include: the toy unit, a USB charging cable (or batteries, depending on model), a quick-start guide, and a warranty card. Some models include additional accessories (charging dock, extra attachments) — refer to the specific product listing for full contents.

## 4. Battery & Maintenance

### 4.1 Power Type
Toys are either **rechargeable via USB-C** or **battery-operated (AA/AAA, not included unless stated)**, depending on the model. This is specified on the product packaging and listing.

### 4.2 Battery Life
Rechargeable toys typically offer **4–8 hours** of active play per full charge, varying by usage intensity (continuous voice/Wi-Fi use drains faster than basic play). A full charge from empty takes approximately **2 hours**.

### 4.3 Cleaning & Maintenance
Wipe the toy's exterior with a soft, slightly damp cloth. Do not submerge electronic components in water. Avoid direct sunlight or high-heat storage, which can damage battery life and plastic components. Fabric-covered toys (if applicable) should be spot-cleaned only unless the manual states they are machine-washable.

## 8. Emotional & Panic Queries — Escalation Triggers

These are not answered from the knowledge base. They are **detected by tone/urgency/safety signal**, not by keyword lookup, and must override the normal RAG response flow — the bot should never try to "answer" these from documents, only acknowledge, reassure briefly, and escalate.

### 8.1 Seriousness Levels & Routing (3-Level System)

| Level | Label | Bot Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Low | **Console the customer directly** — empathetic response, no escalation email sent, bot resolves or reassures in-chat |
| 2 | Medium | **Escalate to Store Manager** — escalation email sent, customer told a manager will follow up |
| 3 | High | **Escalate to CEO/Owner** — escalation email sent immediately, treated as top priority |

### 8.2 Sample Panic/Emotional Queries by Seriousness Level

**Level 1 — Low** *(bot consoles, no escalation needed)*
- "I've had this toy for 2 weeks and it's just not doing what the ad said it would."
- "This is confusing, nothing in the manual makes sense to me, I'm getting frustrated."
- "I don't understand how to even turn this on, I've tried everything and I'm annoyed."
- "My kid is disappointed the toy doesn't look exactly like the picture."

*Bot behavior:* acknowledge the frustration warmly, then attempt to actually help using the knowledge base (setup steps, feature explanation, etc.). Only escalate to Level 2 if, after consoling, the underlying issue still can't be resolved from the documents.

**Level 2 — Medium** *(escalate to Store Manager)*
- "I emailed twice already about my broken toy and no one replied. This is really frustrating."
- "This is the third time I'm asking about my order and I still have no answer."
- "The toy stopped working after 2 days and I paid full price for it, I want this fixed."
- "I've been waiting for days, this is unacceptable, I need someone to actually help me."
- "I'm not happy with this purchase at all and I want a refund or replacement."

*Bot behavior:* empathize, confirm it's being escalated to the store manager, set expectation on follow-up.

**Level 3 — High** *(escalate to CEO/Owner, treated as urgent)*
- "My child choked on a small part that came off the toy."
- "The toy caught fire / started smoking while charging."
- "My daughter had an allergic reaction to the material on this toy."
- "This toy has exposed wiring and my child got a small electric shock."
- "The toy's battery got extremely hot while charging and started melting."
- "I am contacting a lawyer / reporting this to consumer safety authorities."

*Bot behavior:* no troubleshooting attempts — acknowledge, give a basic immediate-safety instruction if relevant (e.g., "please stop using the toy and unplug it now"), and confirm it's being escalated to the owner immediately.

### 8.3 Detection Guidance for the Seriousness Assessment Model

The AI should weigh these factors together, not any single one in isolation:
- **Direct safety/injury language** (choke, shock, fire, burn, allergic reaction, swallowed, melting) → automatic floor of **Level 3**, regardless of the customer's tone — a calmly-worded injury report is still High.
- **Repeated unresolved contact** ("third time," "no one replied," "still waiting") → pushes from Level 1 toward Level 2.
- **Explicit demand for a refund/replacement after a failed product**, without safety language → Level 2.
- **Legal/regulatory language** ("lawyer," "consumer court," "reporting this") → automatic Level 3.
- **General confusion, mild dissatisfaction, or a "how do I" question asked with frustration** → Level 1; the bot should still try to resolve it from the knowledge base after consoling, not escalate reflexively just because the customer sounded annoyed.

### 8.4 Borderline / Ambiguous Cases

These are genuinely hard to classify and worth documenting explicitly, since your seriousness model will be judged on how well it handles them — not just the obvious examples above.

- **"This toy is a fire hazard, I read reviews saying it overheats"** (no personal incident, just concern) — this is a safety *question*, not a safety *event*. Treat as Level 1–2: answer from documentation if certification/safety info exists, but if the customer is clearly anxious and documentation doesn't fully reassure them, escalate to Level 2 rather than Level 3 — there's no injury or active incident yet.
- **"My child says the toy shocked them a little, but they're fine now"** — even a *resolved* or minor safety event should route to Level 3, not be downgraded because the outcome was okay. The incident itself, not the outcome, determines the level.
- **Calm but repeated complaints with no explicit refund demand** ("This is the fourth email, still no update, quite disappointing") — length/repetition alone, without anger or a direct ask, should still reach Level 2 once contact attempts exceed 2–3, since repeated silence is itself a service failure.
- **Sarcasm or passive-aggressive tone without explicit complaint content** ("Wow, great service, really impressed 👍") — don't rely on literal wording; if sentiment is clearly negative despite polite phrasing, treat as at least Level 1 (console) and check conversation history for an underlying unresolved issue before deciding if it's actually Level 2.
- **A parent asking hypothetically** ("What happens if this catches fire?") vs. **reporting it happening** — hypothetical/preventive questions stay Level 1 and should be answered from safety/certification documentation; only an actual reported event triggers Level 3.

### 8.5 Bot Response Pattern by Level
1. **Level 1:** Empathetic acknowledgment → attempt real resolution from knowledge base → no escalation email unless resolution fails.
2. **Level 2:** Empathetic acknowledgment → confirm escalation to store manager → escalation email sent with transcript, reason, timestamp.
3. **Level 3:** Brief acknowledgment (no over-apologizing) → basic immediate-safety instruction if applicable → confirm escalation to CEO/Owner as top priority → escalation email sent immediately with full transcript, reason, timestamp.

## 9. Conversational Workflow Spec
*(This section defines chatbot behavior/flow — not retrievable content. Keep it out of the RAG index; it belongs in application logic / system prompt, similar to the guardrails doc.)*

### 9.1 Entry Point — Greeting Trigger
Any greeting-type message ("Hi," "Hello," "Hey") triggers a fixed, hardcoded menu — not a RAG-retrieved answer.

```
Bot: Hello! 👋 I'm here to help with anything about your smart toy.
Choose a topic to get started, or just type your question anytime:
1. Connectivity & Setup
2. Features & How It Works
3. Battery & Maintenance
4. Pricing & Discounts
5. Orders, Delivery, Warranty & Safety
```

### 9.2 Submenu on Selection
Selecting a top-level number shows its subpoints, mapped directly to existing KB subsections so the menu and retrieval index stay in sync (no separate content to maintain).

Example — selecting "1":
```
1.1 Which frequency band do I connect to?
1.2 Bluetooth or Wi-Fi — which does my toy use?
1.3 How do I pair it for the first time?
1.4 Pairing isn't working — help
1.5 How do I factory reset it?
```
Selecting a subpoint (e.g., "1.3") retrieves that exact chunk and answers directly.

### 9.3 Free-Text Is Always Open
The menu is a shortcut, not a gate. From the very first message, the customer can type any question directly and skip the menu entirely — if the first message is already a real question, the bot answers it directly without ever showing the menu.

### 9.4 Answer-First Default
For every message going forward, the bot's default behavior is to attempt an answer from the knowledge base first. Escalation is the exception path, triggered only by the seriousness assessment (Section 8) — not by default, and not just because a question falls outside the numbered menu.

### 9.5 Escalation Applies Identically Regardless of Entry Path
Menu-driven and free-text-driven queries feed into the same escalation logic — there is no separate rule for either path. A customer navigating the menu who then reports a safety incident mid-flow hits the same Level 3 floor as if they'd typed it directly; menu context never overrides the seriousness assessment.

- **Level 1:** bot consoles + resolves in-chat, no escalation.
- **Level 2:** escalate to Store Manager.
- **Level 3:** escalate to CEO/Owner — automatic safety floor overrides all menu/conversation context.
